\begin{abstract}
Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has become the standard
reinforcement-learning recipe for fine-tuning reasoning language models,
but it relies on a hidden assumption: the $K$ rollouts in each group
must differ, or the group-mean advantage $A^{(k)} = r^{(k)} - \mu_r$
collapses to zero.  Latent-reasoning models such as \textsc{Coconut},
which feed continuous hidden states recurrently in place of discrete
chain-of-thought tokens, violate this assumption --- conditional on the
parameters and prompt, the latent phase is deterministic, so all $K$
rollouts produce identical trajectories and GRPO makes no progress.

We propose to source the missing stochasticity from \emph{dropout}.
Each rollout is run with one Bernoulli mask drawn afresh and held
constant across all latent recurrence steps (variational dropout in the
sense of Gal \& Ghahramani, 2016); the mask is recorded and replayed
during the policy update, so the trajectory that was graded is
reproduced bit-identically.  We prove that, under the mean-only
advantage $A^{(k)} = r^{(k)} - \mu_r$ (without standard-deviation
normalization, following Liu et al., 2024), the resulting surrogate
gradient equals $(1 - 1/K)\,\nabla J(\theta)$ exactly, with $J$ the
expected reward of the mask-marginalized policy --- which is itself a
Bayesian model average over the variational dropout posterior.  Mask
replay further provides a common-random-numbers coupling that
lower-bounds the variance of a fresh-rollout estimator throughout the
PPO trust region.

We document four implementation refinements that prove necessary in
practice: a Huberized $k_3$ KL estimator with bounded but nonzero tail
gradient, a learning-rate-coupled annealing of the KL coefficient,
sequence-mean rather than token-mean loss aggregation, and a group-level
accuracy filter that drops uninformative groups before any backward
pass and acts as an emergent online curriculum.  On math-reasoning
fine-tuning, dropout-GRPO with mask replay trains stably where the
deterministic-rollout baseline does not, and we release a reference
implementation that realizes bit-identical mask replay across the entire
latent recurrence.
\end{abstract}
