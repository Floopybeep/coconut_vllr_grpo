❯ I have a research idea that I am trying to formulate using concrete           
  mathematical proofs, but I am having some trouble.                            
  My idea is using dropout as a stochasticity generator for latent reasoning    
  LLM model fine-tuning, specifically GRPO on math datasets.                    
  The latent reasoning model in question follows the Coconut model from Hao et  
  al, which uses the previous time-step's hidden states as input to the next    
  time-step during latent reasoning.                                            
  The problem here is that latent reasoning steps are deterministic in nature,  
  so you cannot just sample the next token as you would in language modeling.   
  To resolve this, I am proposing to use a small dropout(~10%) during rollout   
  generation in order to generate a diverse hidden state.                       
  The dropout-generated answers will be graded and ranked to group-normalized   
  advantages.                                                                   
  During the policy update, the exact same dropout mask will be applied to the  
  model to replicate the exact same hidden states during generation, and then   
  backpropagated accordingly whether the latent trajectory generated a correct  
  answer or not.                                                                
                                                                                
  My initial hypothesis was that this can be modeled as a group of similar      
  parameters to the "true" model parameters, but I am encountering trouble      
  proceeding from this point.                                                   
  Help me formulate a concrete mathematical induction that demonstrates a       
  viable proof that this technique will work.                                   

● Here's framework. Math kept rigorous; prose compressed.

  1. Formal setup

  Let model params θ. Dropout mask ξ ~ p(ξ), independent of θ. Define perturbed
  params:

  $$\tilde\theta(\xi) = \theta \odot m(\xi)/q$$

  where m(ξ) ∈ {0,1}^d Bernoulli(q), so E_ξ[m(ξ)/q] = 1.

  Coconut latent recurrence with dropout, t = 1,...,T:

  $$h_t = f_{\tilde\theta_t(\xi_t)}(h_{t-1}), \quad h_0 = \text{embed}(x)$$

  Final answer tokens y_1,...,y_L sampled from π_θ(· | x, h_T) standard
  autoregressive (token sampling stochastic; can fold into ξ too).

  Joint noise: ξ = (ξ_1, ..., ξ_T). Trajectory τ = (h_1,...,h_T, y_1,...,y_L).

  2. Key reframing — ξ as exogenous noise (reparameterization)

  Crucial point: p(ξ) does not depend on θ. So policy factors as

  $$\pi_\theta(\tau, \xi \mid x) = p(\xi),\pi_\theta(\tau \mid x,\xi)$$

  Given ξ fixed, latent path h_{1:T} is deterministic function of θ. Only
  token-level y_{1:L} retains stochasticity (or also reparameterized via Gumbel
  if you fold sampling noise into ξ).

  This is exactly the reparameterization trick (Kingma & Welling 2014) / common 
  random numbers (CRN, Glasserman & Yao 1992) setup. Replaying same ξ at
  policy-update time = CRN variance reduction.

  3. Induction on latent depth T

  Claim: ∇_θ log π_θ(y | x, ξ) is well-defined and computable via backprop
  through the unrolled latent graph for any T, when ξ is fixed.

  Base (T=0): No latent steps. Standard token policy:
  $$\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y\mid x) = \sum_\ell \nabla_\theta \log
  \pi_\theta(y_\ell \mid x, y_{<\ell})$$
  Standard, holds.

  Inductive step: Assume well-defined for T−1 latent steps. For T steps,
  $$h_T = f_{\tilde\theta_T(\xi_T)}(h_{T-1}(\theta, \xi_{<T}, x))$$
  Chain rule:
  $$\frac{\partial h_T}{\partial \theta} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial
  f}{\partial \tilde\theta_T}\frac{\partial \tilde\theta_T}{\partial
  \theta}}{\text{direct, mask-scaled}} + \underbrace{\frac{\partial f}{\partial 
  h{T-1}}\frac{\partial h_{T-1}}{\partial \theta}}_{\text{recursive, by IH}}$$

  Both terms exist; ∂h_{T-1}/∂θ exists by inductive hypothesis. Token log-probs
  are smooth in h_T. Thus

  $$\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y \mid x, \xi) = \sum_\ell \nabla_{h_T}
  \log\pi_\theta(y_\ell \mid \cdot),\frac{\partial h_T}{\partial \theta} +
  \nabla_\theta^{\text{direct}}\log\pi_\theta(y_\ell\mid\cdot)$$

  is well-defined for all T. ∎

  4. Unbiasedness of dropout-GRPO

  Define ξ-conditional surrogate (PPO/GRPO clipped form):

  $$\mathcal{L}^{\text{GRPO}}(\theta;\xi,y,x) = \min!\Big(\rho_\theta(\xi,y) A,\
   \text{clip}(\rho_\theta,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A\Big)$$

  with importance ratio
  $$\rho_\theta(\xi,y) = \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid
  x,\xi)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y\mid x,\xi)}$$

  and group-normalized advantage A computed from rewards of group of K rollouts
  {(ξ^k, y^k)} sharing x.

  Theorem (unbiased policy gradient under CRN replay): At θ = θ_old,

  $$\mathbb{E}{\xi,y}\big[\nabla\theta
  \mathcal{L}^{\text{GRPO}}\big]\Big|{\theta=\theta\text{old}} =
  \mathbb{E}{\xi,y}\big[A \cdot \nabla\theta \log \pi_\theta(y\mid x,\xi)\big]$$

  which equals ∇_θ J(θ) for the augmented policy

  $$\Pi_\theta(y,\xi\mid x) := p(\xi),\pi_\theta(y\mid x,\xi)$$

  since ∇_θ log p(ξ) = 0.

  Why replay matters (variance): Without replay you compute ρ_θ using a
  different ξ' than rollout — ratio numerator and denominator differ in two
  sources (θ change AND noise change). With replay,
  $$\text{Var}[\hat g_{\text{CRN}}] \le \text{Var}[\hat g_{\text{indep}}]$$
  by standard CRN coupling argument: covariance term is positive when r(τ) is
  monotone in shared randomness.

  5. The "group of similar parameters" formalization

  Your intuition maps cleanly onto Bayesian-dropout view (Gal & Ghahramani
  2016): each ξ defines posterior sample θ̃(ξ). The augmented policy is the
  marginal

  $$\pi_\theta(y\mid x) = \int p(\xi)\pi_\theta(y\mid x,\xi),d\xi =
  \mathbb{E}{\theta'\sim q\theta}[\pi_{\theta'}(y\mid x)]$$

  GRPO with replayed dropout = per-sample MAP gradient within ensemble:

  $$\nabla_\theta J = \mathbb{E}{\xi}\big[\nabla\theta
  J(\tilde\theta(\xi))\cdot\nabla_\theta\tilde\theta(\xi)\big]$$

  Group normalization across K different ξ samples = Monte Carlo estimate over
  ensemble members, with control-variate baseline = group mean reward.

  6. What still needs care (gaps to close)

  - Token-distribution mode collapse: if final token sampling is the only true
  stochastic source, dropout perturbations to h_T may not change argmax → zero
  gradient signal on many rollouts. Verify h_T sensitivity empirically.
  - Ratio explosion: ρ_θ(ξ,y) under dropout can be large because dropping
  different units changes logits non-smoothly. Clipping ε may need tightening;
  consider per-step ratio clipping along latent path.
  - CRN validity for clip operator: clip is non-smooth. Theorem 4 holds at
  θ=θ_old (initial step) but needs Lipschitz argument for trust-region steps.
  Standard PPO analysis (Schulman 2017 §5) extends if dropout-induced ratio
  stays bounded.
  - Bias from MC-dropout marginalization: during evaluation/test you typically
  turn dropout off. Mismatch between train marginal Π_θ and deploy π_θ(no
  dropout). Either (a) keep dropout at test, (b) bound TV-distance ‖Π_θ −
  π_θ‖_TV ≤ O(p√d) and accept bias.

  Suggested write-up structure

  1. Defs (Coconut recurrence + dropout perturbation)
  2. Lemma: ξ exogenous → reparameterization applies
  3. Induction lemma (§3) — gradient well-defined for any T
  4. Theorem (§4) — unbiased GRPO surrogate gradient under CRN replay
  5. Corollary — variance reduction vs. independent-noise estimator
  6. Connection to MC-dropout posterior (§5)
  7. Discussion of gaps (§6)

  Divergences

  D1. Ref model conflates θ_old (ratio denom) and π_ref (KL anchor) — 
  significant

  Draft eq 4.7 has two distinct frozen models:
  - ρ_θ = π_θ / π_{θ_old} where θ_old = rollout-time policy
  - KL(π_θ ‖ π_ref) where π_ref = separate anchor (e.g., SFT init)

  Code uses single ref_model for both, refreshed every ref_model_renewal_steps
  (768-778). Consequences:
  - Theorem 4.4 ("ρ_{θ_old} ≡ 1 at update start") no longer holds: ratio denom
  is N-steps-stale snapshot, not start-of-step θ.
  - KL anchor drifts toward current policy → KL regularization weakens over
  training.
  - Single mini-epoch (E=1) means clip is mostly cosmetic only when refresh=1;
  with larger refresh, this becomes off-policy PPO without proper θ_old logging.

  Fix options: (a) log pi_θ_old snapshot at rollout time (cache mb_ref_lp from
  rollout-time forward), keep separate frozen pi_ref; (b) set
  ref_model_renewal_steps=1 to recover on-policy ratio (loses KL anchor
  entirely).

  D2. Default reference_model_mode="deterministic" breaks CRN premise — 
  significant

  Lines 443, 960-965: default puts ref_model.eval() → dropout OFF when computing
   mb_ref_lp. Then ratio is

  ρ = π_θ(y | x, ξ) / π_ref(y | x, no dropout)

  Two different conditional distributions. Violates draft eq 4.6 which assumes
  same ξ in numerator and denominator. CRN coupling (Prop 4.5) does not apply to
   this ratio.

  Code DOES expose reference_model_mode="dropout_matched" (960-961, 969-970)
  which restores rollout rng_state on ref forward — this branch IS faithful to
  draft. But it's opt-in.

  Fix: switch default to dropout_matched, or document that deterministic mode is
   off-spec.

  D3. KL estimator computes wrong distribution under D2

  mb_kl = mb_ratio - log_ratio - 1 (1056) is k3 estimator of KL(p‖q) where
  p=π_θ(·|x,ξ), q=π_ref(·|x,ξ_ref). When ξ_ref ≠ ξ (default deterministic mode),
   this is not KL on the augmented policy Π_θ — biases regularizer. Theorem 4.4
  corollary ("KL on augmented") fails.

  D4. Hard clamp log_ratio.clamp(-5, 5) (1045) — undocumented

  Draft §6 mentions ratio explosion as caveat but doesn't formalize defense.
  Code adds aggressive exp(±5) = [0.0067, 148] clamp before PPO ε-clip. Fine in
  practice, just absent from theory writeup.

  D5. <|end-latent|> included in policy log-prob — minor

  compute_log_probs (276-301) uses response_start = prompt_len + num_latents →
  first token graded is <|end-latent|> (since latents occupy positions
  prompt_len ... prompt_len+num_latents-1). Draft eq 2.6 grades only y_1...y_L
  (post-latent answer tokens). Code grades <|end-latent|> + answer tokens.
  Defensible — <|end-latent|> IS sampled, not deterministic — but worth noting
  in writeup as part of "answer phase".

  D6. Format penalty / length penalty extend draft reward — minor

  Draft says r ∈ [0,1]. Code: format_penalty=-0.5,
  length_penalty_per_token=0.01·max(0, n_lat-1). Doesn't break theory (A still
  well-defined for arbitrary scalar r) but drifts from clean r ∈ [0,1] setup.

  D7. Single mini-epoch (E=1) — minor

  Draft Algorithm 1.c specifies "mini-epoch e=1,…,E". Code does single pass over
   policy_mb_size chunks. With D1 (stale ref), this means ratio at start of step
   is ≠ 1, but only one update is taken before next rollout. Consistent with
  practical GRPO; just diverges from draft's E loop.

  D8. configure_dropout coverage — verify

  Lines 405-415 set 8 attribute names. Some Transformers (e.g., GPT-NeoX, modern
   LLaMA configs) may use other names (mlp_dropout, hidden_act_dropout, etc.).
  Worth grep on the actual model_config post-load to confirm dropout is non-zero
   everywhere expected.

  Summary recommendation

  Two real divergences worth fixing or documenting:

  1. Ref-model role conflation (D1) — break ref into theta_old (logged at
  rollout, used for ratio) and pi_ref (frozen SFT, used for KL). This restores
  Theorem 4.4 ρ≡1 condition.
  2. Default ref mode (D2/D3) — flip default to dropout_matched, otherwise the
  CRN proof (Prop 4.5) does not describe the running code.

  Everything else is faithful or minor. The empirical embed-match assertion
  (assert_rollout_replay_embeddings_match) is a strong sanity check that the
  latent trajectory replay is bit-exact — confirms Lemma 4.2 implementation
  works as intended.

● Not quite — two separate mechanisms, two separate anchors. Let me untangle.

  Two anchors, two roles

  PPO ratio (clip term) — denom = θ_old (policy just before this step's gradient
   update):

  $$\rho = \frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}}, \qquad \text{clip}(\rho,
  1-\varepsilon, 1+\varepsilon)$$

  Purpose: trust region on a single update step. Bounds how far one gradient
  step can move the policy distribution. Local.

  KL term — anchor = π_ref (frozen reference, e.g. SFT init), unchanged from
  base code:

  $$\beta \cdot \text{KL}(\pi_\theta ,|, \pi_\text{ref})$$

  Purpose: prevents cumulative drift over the entire training run away from the
  well-behaved SFT model. Global.

  Mapping to the code

  ┌─────────────────────┬───────────────┬───────────────────────────┬───────┐
  │        Term         │   Numerator   │        Denominator        │ Lines │
  ├─────────────────────┼───────────────┼───────────────────────────┼───────┤
  │ mb_log_ratio (PPO   │ new pi_θ      │ θ_old (rollout-time,      │ 1101  │
  │ clip)               │ log-prob      │ fresh each step)          │       │
  ├─────────────────────┼───────────────┼───────────────────────────┼───────┤
  │ mb_log_ratio_ref    │ new pi_θ      │ π_ref (frozen,            │ 1110  │
  │ (KL)                │ log-prob      │ refresh-able)             │       │
  └─────────────────────┴───────────────┴───────────────────────────┴───────┘

  Both ratios share the same numerator (mb_new_lp). Different denominators.

  Why this fixes the original

  Original code: mb_ref_lp was used for both ratio denom AND KL anchor → ratio
  was vs. an N-step-stale snapshot, not vs. θ_old. Theorem 4.4's "ρ ≡ 1 at step
  start" failed.

  Fix: capture θ_old log-probs fresh at every rollout (matched dropout RNG) → ρ
  ≡ 1 at step start → clip is a true per-step trust region. KL still anchors to
  π_ref, just like DeepSeekMath / standard GRPO.

  Restating your sentence corrected

  ▎ "By clipping to the policy model before the update, we prevent the model 
  ▎ from diverging too far from what it should have generated"

  Replace "diverging" with "stepping". Clip prevents the one gradient step from
  moving π_θ too far from π_{θ_old}. KL (separate term) prevents cumulative 
  training from moving π_θ too far from π_ref. Different time scales, different
  anchors.
